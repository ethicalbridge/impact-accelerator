Proportionate to our size

Handova is in its early stage. Today one person, the founder, runs both Handova and Ethical Bridge. This is our first version: it sets out what we do now and who does it. We will expand it as Handova grows and builds a team with dedicated safeguarding and compliance roles. Until then:

  • The founder is the safeguarding and privacy lead. She receives reports, decides on protective action and keeps the records described here.
  • An independent contact handles any concern about the founder, or that the founder is involved in, and covers urgent cases when the founder can't be reached for more than 48 hours. This is a trusted person outside Handova and Ethical Bridge: to be confirmed soon. They don't run Handova or investigate alone: they receive the concern, make sure it is handled fairly and get outside advice when needed.
  • We review this policy at least once a year, as soon as Handova has a team, and after any serious incident.

At a glance

  • We collect only what Handova needs to match professionals with non-profit organisations, record what they agreed and keep people safe.
  • No beneficiary personal data on Handova by default. Needs, profiles and messages must not contain names, case files or identity documents of the people an organisation serves.
  • If an organisation shares personal data with a professional for a contribution, the organisation is responsible for it (the "controller"), as the contribution agreement says.
  • Identity checks use Didit and biometric data, so they run only with explicit, recorded consent. Handova keeps only the outcome and the name on the document.
  • The administrator uses two-step sign-in, sees only what each task needs, and reads a conversation only once it has been reported.
  • Signed agreements and contribution records are kept for six years after an account closes, then deleted. Everything else has a shorter, stated period.
  • We answer every privacy request within one month and log it.
  • Report any suspected data breach immediately to hello@handova.org. We decide within 72 hours whether to notify the relevant supervisory authority.

Who this applies to

  • The Handova team: today, the founder, who is the only administrator; in future, any administrator, volunteer, intern or contractor who can see Handova data.
  • Ethical Bridge LLC, as operator of Handova.
  • Service providers that process personal data for Handova (listed in Annex A).
  • Professionals and organisations, for the data rules in the agreements they sign. Their own duties are set out in those agreements; this policy explains how Handova supports them.

1. Purpose and scope

People trust Handova with their profiles, identity, work and concerns. This policy covers all personal data processed for Handova, in any form: database, file storage, email, spreadsheets, notes and screenshots.

Legal framework. Ethical Bridge LLC is registered in Wyoming, USA, and is subject to US federal and Wyoming law. People take part from many countries, so we also follow the law that applies where they are, including the EU and UK GDPR for personal data. Where rules differ, we follow the one that gives people more protection. In practice we work to the GDPR standard throughout, because many users are in the EU and our database is hosted in Frankfurt. Ethical Bridge LLC appoints an EU representative where Article 27 GDPR requires it. Where we must notify an authority, we notify the relevant supervisory authority.

3. Principles

All processing follows these principles, and we keep records that show it.

  1. Lawful, fair and open. Every use has a lawful basis and is explained in the privacy notice.
  2. Purpose limitation. Data collected for matching, agreements or safety is not reused for unrelated purposes, such as advertising. We never sell data.
  3. Data minimisation. We collect only what each step needs. We prefer totals, ID numbers and anonymised data.
  4. Accuracy. People can see and correct their own profile at any time. Organisations review endorsements before they count, and professionals can request corrections.
  5. Storage limitation. Data is deleted on the schedule in section 9 and Annex A.
  6. Security. Data is protected by the measures in section 8.
  7. Accountability. We document our choices: Annex A (record of processing), Annex B (DPIA), Annex C (breach procedure), the rights request log and the breach log.

5. Data minimisation and privacy by design

  • No beneficiary personal data by default. Every need confirms it involves no direct contact with children or adults at risk and no access to their personal information. Handova reviews every need before it opens and asks for changes if it contains personal details.
  • Profiles, needs and messages must not include beneficiary names, case files, identity documents or confidential research.
  • Notification emails never include what people write in Messages or the free-text reasons people give.
  • Profiles are private until the person publishes them and Handova approves them. Impact CV entries are public only if the professional chooses.
  • Questionnaire answers, career stage and "would you work together again" answers are visible only to Handova and are reported only as totals.
  • Handova stores the outcome of an identity check and the name on the document, never the document or selfie.
  • Before we launch a new feature, data set or provider, the privacy lead screens it for privacy risk and updates Annex A. A DPIA is required for processing likely to be high risk, including biometric checks, profiling or ranking people, AI that assesses people or content, and any processing of children's data or data about adults at risk.

6. Personal data shared within a contribution

Sometimes a contribution cannot be done with anonymised data. Then the contribution agreement applies:

  • The organisation is the controller for any personal data it shares. It shares data only when anonymised data will not do, and only if it may lawfully share it with the professional in their country.
  • The professional acts as a volunteer under the organisation's authority, uses the data only on its instructions, and deletes or returns it at handover, confirming this in Messages.
  • Data is shared through a secure channel the organisation chooses, never through Handova. Passwords, card details, PINs and access codes are never shared on Handova. System access uses individual, limited accounts removed when the work ends.
  • Personal or confidential data never goes into AI tools, and never into personal accounts without the organisation's written permission.
  • Either party reports a suspected breach to the other and to Handova within 24 hours. The organisation decides on notification as controller; Handova helps (Annex C).

Handova does not see or store this data and is not a party to the contribution.

7. Sharing, processors and international transfers

Other users. Handova connects people across countries. A published profile can be seen by anyone, anywhere. Applications, messages and agreements are seen by the members of the organisation concerned, who may be in another country. We explain this in the privacy notice before people publish or apply. The legal basis for these user-to-user transfers is that they are necessary for the contract the person asked for: publishing a profile or applying.

Processors. Before using a provider that processes personal data for Handova, the privacy lead checks its security, makes sure a data processing agreement is in place, records where it processes data, and makes sure data is deleted at the end of the service. Current providers and their agreements are in Annex A.

Transfers outside the EEA. We transfer personal data to a provider outside the EEA only with an adequacy decision (including the EU–US Data Privacy Framework for certified companies) or standard contractual clauses with a transfer risk assessment. Annex A records the safeguard for each provider.

Authorities. We report to the relevant authorities where the law requires it, or where someone is in immediate danger. The privacy lead decides, records the reason and shares the minimum.

8. Security

Administrator access - Every administrator account uses two-step sign-in. - Administrators get the least access their task needs. Access is reviewed every six months and removed the day someone leaves the role. - Administrators read a conversation only once it, or its contribution, has been reported. The platform enforces this. - Only administrators see the name on an identity document, signature evidence (email, IP address, browser) and report case notes. - Admin actions (suspensions, take-downs, frozen conversations, closed contributions, report decisions) are logged. - Direct database or dashboard access (Supabase) is limited to the privacy lead, used only for maintenance or an incident, and recorded in the admin log. - Secret keys (database service key, Didit, email) are stored only as provider secrets, never in code or documents, and rotated after any suspected exposure.

Everyone with access - Use strong, unique passwords and two-step sign-in on every work account, including email. - Use updated, encrypted devices with screen lock. - Copy Handova data out of the platform only when the task needs it; delete local copies when done. - Never put personal, confidential, report, identity or safeguarding information into AI tools. - Report phishing or anything suspicious straight away.

Platform measures. Row-level security controls who can read each record. Messages cannot be edited or deleted. The site uses HTTPS. Sign-in and identity checks are rate-limited.

9. Retention

We keep data only as long as needed. The full schedule is in Annex A. In short:

  • Account and profile: until the person deletes the account. Profiles closed for inactivity are kept unpublished so they can be reopened, and are deleted after 24 months without sign-in, after a reminder.
  • Signed platform and contribution agreements, with signature evidence: six years after the account closes, then deleted automatically.
  • Contribution records (need, hours, deliverables, endorsement, questionnaire answers), kept by the organisation with the professional shown as "Former member": six years after the account closes, then deleted.
  • Messages: deleted with the account, except in conversations linked to a contribution or a report, which are kept six years after the last message.
  • Identity checks: Handova's record is deleted with the account. Didit keeps the session, including images, for up to one year under our current setting, and deletes it earlier when the account is deleted.
  • Reports of concern: six years after the case closes, or longer if legal proceedings are ongoing or expected. Cases involving a child are kept until the child turns 25 or for six years after closure, whichever is later. Then deleted. Reviewed once a year. Only the safeguarding lead, and the independent contact for cases about the founder, can see them.
  • Notification email log: 12 months.
  • Admin action log: six years.
  • Rights requests and breach records: three years after closure, stored in Handova's secure records.
  • Analytics: event data two months; cookies up to two years unless withdrawn.

At each annual review the privacy lead checks that automatic deletion jobs ran.

10. Rights and how we handle requests

People can ask to access, correct, erase, restrict or port their data, object to its use, withdraw consent, and ask for human review of any automated decision. They can complain to the data protection authority where they live or work.

Many rights are self-service: people can edit or unpublish their profile, turn off emails and delete their account from Account and privacy. Other requests go to hello@handova.org.

How we handle a request 1. The privacy lead logs it: date received, person, request, deadline, actions, date closed. 2. We confirm identity proportionately, normally by replying to the account's email address. We do not ask for ID documents for this. 3. We respond within one month, free of charge. For complex requests we may extend by up to two months and tell the person why within the first month. 4. We refuse only manifestly unfounded or excessive requests, and explain how to complain. 5. If erasure is limited (for example signed agreements kept six years), we say what we keep, why and until when. 6. If a request concerns data an organisation controls under a contribution, we pass it to that organisation and tell the person. 7. If a request concerns the privacy lead herself, the independent contact (to be confirmed soon) oversees it.

We will expand this procedure as Handova grows.

11. Automated decisions and AI

Handova makes no significant decision about anyone by automated means alone. Automatic checks (such as the name match in the identity check, or the inactivity rule) only flag cases or send reminders; a person reviews every rejection, removal or suspension. Where Handova restricts or removes an account or content, we tell the person why and how to ask for a review. Personal, confidential, report, identity or safeguarding information is never entered into AI tools.

12. Children

Handova is for people aged 18 and over; professionals confirm their age before publishing. We do not knowingly collect children's data. If a user is under 18, we close the account and delete the data, keeping only what a safeguarding concern needs. A need involving children's personal data is taken down and treated as a safeguarding concern.

13. Email, notifications and cookies

Notification emails are part of the service and can be turned off in Account and privacy. Newsletters need prior opt-in and an easy unsubscribe. Google Analytics loads only after "Accept analytics"; declining is as easy as accepting, and the choice can be changed at any time.

14. Personal data breaches

Anyone who suspects a breach reports it immediately, and within 24 hours at the latest, to hello@handova.org. The privacy lead follows Annex C, records every breach, and decides within 72 hours whether to notify the relevant supervisory authority and the people affected.

15. Obligations of the team, volunteers and contractors

Everyone with access to Handova data signs a confidentiality commitment (which continues after they leave), uses data only for their task, follows section 8, and reports breaches, concerns and rights requests the same day. Breaches of this policy can lead to removal of access and of the role. Concerns about misuse of data can also be raised under the Whistleblowing Policy and Procedure, at safeguarding@handova.org.

For the future. As Handova grows, we will add training for the team and short briefings for professionals and organisations.

Annex B: Data protection impact assessment — Didit identity check

Status: approved with conditions by Julieta Castiñeira de Dios, 20 October 2026. Decision: approved with conditions (the mitigations in section 5). Next review: October 2027, or when the workflow or retention changes.

1. What the processing is

Before a first contribution agreement, a professional photographs a passport or ID card and records a live selfie on Didit's hosted page. Didit compares the faces (biometric data) and reads the document. Handova stores only the session ID, outcome, date and document name, and compares that name with the profile name; a mismatch goes to the administrator. Others see only an "ID verified" badge. Didit keeps sessions, including images, for up to one year; account deletion deletes them first.

2. Why a DPIA is needed

Biometric data for identification is special category data. The check is systematic, applies to people in many countries, and is a condition for taking part.

3. Necessity

Purpose: give organisations confidence that a professional is who they say they are; stop fake profiles; make it harder for someone removed for misconduct to return under another name.

Alternatives considered - LinkedIn and profile review only (already done): easy to fake. - Document without selfie: does not show the person holds it. - Video call with the administrator viewing the document: no biometrics, but takes time. Suitable for people who do not consent. - No check: falls short of the trust organisations need.

The automated check with a human alternative is the least intrusive option that achieves the purpose at scale.

4. Proportionality

  • Only professionals, only once, only before their first contribution agreement.
  • Handova never stores images, document numbers or dates of birth.
  • Only the administrator sees the document name.
  • Data is used only for identity assurance and related safety reviews, never for other purposes.
  • Clear explanation and a consent screen before people reach Didit.

5. Risks and mitigations

Risk to people Likelihood / severity Mitigations Residual
Consent not freely given, because the check is required to sign Likely / medium Offer a video-call alternative with the administrator; say so on the consent screen and in the privacy notice Low
Consent cannot be proven Likely / medium Record consent (time, notice version, wording shown) when a person selects "I consent"; consent can be withdrawn by email, triggering deletion of the Didit session Low

6. Residual risk and decision

With the mitigations above in place, residual risk is assessed as low to medium. Prior consultation with a supervisory authority is not needed, because the residual risk is not high. Until consent recording and the video-call alternative exist, the risk is higher and the check must not be the only route to signing.

Annex C: Personal data breach procedure

What counts as a breach

A breach is any security incident that leads to personal data being lost, destroyed, changed, disclosed or accessed without permission, or becoming unavailable. Examples: a platform bug that shows private answers or someone else's messages; an administrator or email account taken over; a leaked service key; an email sent to the wrong person; a breach at Supabase, Didit, Hostinger, Google or GitHub; a professional losing or misusing personal data an organisation shared for a contribution.

Who decides

The privacy lead decides on containment and notification. If she is unavailable for more than 12 hours, or involved, the independent contact (to be confirmed soon) decides. For serious or complex cases, Handova may bring in an external adviser or investigator. When an organisation is the controller (section 6), it decides on notification; Handova supports it and handles any part involving Handova's own data.

Today the privacy lead carries out every step below herself. We will expand this procedure as Handova grows.

Checklist

First hour - [ ] Whoever discovers it reports it to the privacy lead at once, by email to hello@handova.org. - [ ] Open an entry in the breach log; note the time we became aware. The 72-hour clock starts here. - [ ] Contain: rotate exposed keys or passwords, end sessions, remove access, suspend accounts, freeze conversations, take down content, or disable the affected feature. - [ ] Preserve evidence: keep logs and screenshots; do not delete records. - [ ] If someone is in danger, follow the Safeguarding Policy and Procedure first.

Within 24 hours - [ ] Establish what data, how many people, which countries, and whether special category, identity or safeguarding data is involved. - [ ] Assess the risk to people: identity theft, discrimination, physical harm, distress, loss of confidentiality. - [ ] Tell the processor or controller concerned, and ask for their findings. - [ ] Decide whether notification is needed and prepare messages.

Within 72 hours - [ ] Notify the relevant supervisory authority unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people. If facts are missing, notify what we know and follow up. - [ ] Tell affected people without undue delay when the breach is likely to affect them, as the privacy notice promises, and always when the risk is high. Say what happened, what data, likely effects, what we have done and what they can do, and give a contact. - [ ] Record the decision and the reasons, including decisions not to notify.

Within 30 days - [ ] Close the incident, record lessons and fixes, and update this policy, Annex A or Annex B if needed.
